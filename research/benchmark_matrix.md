### Classical conclusion

The exact dataset is often reported at 98–100% accuracy by flexible classical models. That ceiling is not evidence of clinical readiness; it is a warning that split leakage, single-site artifacts, missingness patterns, label separability and tiny test folds can dominate the result. Our experiment must test sensitivity, specificity and uncertainty under a fully nested pipeline rather than attempt another best-accuracy claim.

## What can and cannot be concluded

- **Can conclude:** CKD+QSVC, CKD+quantum kernels, CKD+VQC/QNN, CKD+feature selection, CKD+PCA, limited noise tests, shot sweeps and composite QML scores have all been attempted.
- **Can conclude:** in the best directly described PCA comparison, classical SVM materially outperformed QSVM and was far faster.
- **Cannot conclude:** any cited QML model has a quantum advantage; most ran on classical simulators, and none establishes asymptotic or hardware speedup.
- **Cannot conclude:** 95–100% values represent clinical performance. The dataset is only 400 single-site rows and several protocols have leakage or reporting ambiguity.
- **Cannot conclude:** a selected field count equals a reduced number of clinical tests or a measured saving. Several fields can arise from the same specimen, and collection workflows/costs were not prospectively measured.
- **Cannot rank across papers:** model values differ by row removal, target encoding, imputation, augmentation, feature selection, split, seed, CV and metric threshold. Our only defensible comparison is paired models within the new, identical pipeline.
